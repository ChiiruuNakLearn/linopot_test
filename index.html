Step 1: Cooking & Infusing

Slide 3 / 8

1 Boil Combined Ingredients

Diced sweet potato and cleaned hill rice are cooked together in the same pot to unify the juices.

2 Natural Color Integration

As the starch bubbles and reaches a boil, the sweet potato melts and naturally bleeds a brilliant purple color into the rice grains.

Step 2: Pounding the Mash

Slide 4 / 8

The Art of "Semi-Mashing"

While the mixture is steaming hot, use a wooden pestle or paddle to stir and press the sweet potato cubes. They must dissolve entirely to act as a sweet, sticky binder.

However, keep some whole rice grains intact! This preserves a robust, rustic bite rather than a baby-food puree.

Crucial detail: The pounding must be executed while the mash is boiling hot, or the starch bonds will fail to lock.

Step 3: Preparing the "Bio-Wrap"

Slide 5 / 8

The Science of Fire-Wilting

Raw jungle Tarap or banana leaves are naturally stiff and brittle. If folded immediately, they split open and leak valuable starch.

By heating the leaves briefly over a burner flame, we soften their structural fibers. This renders the leaf as pliable as modern foil. It also releases natural oils that season the hot rice.

  • Softens leaf fibers for wrapping
  • Activates fragrant herbal essential oils

Step 4: Molding & Folding

Slide 6 / 8

Stage: Placed Core Mash

The Science of the Vacuum Seal

Position your piping-hot rice ball in the center of the wilted leaf. By overlapping the left and right flaps, and folding the excess bottom flaps underneath, you trap the hot steam.

As the rice cools, it creates a vacuum effect. The wrapping contracts and holds itself closed. **Zero rope, tape, or rubber bands are required!**

Eco Heritage Slide 8 / 8

Ancient Technology for a Modern Future

Long before plastics, cling wrap, and modern lunch boxes, the native communities of Borneo had successfully engineered a zero-waste, 100% biodegradable, heat-retaining food carrier.

Studying Linopot allows us to recognize the ecological intelligence embedded in historical Southeast Asian customs, reminding us that sustainability is a ancient tradition.
